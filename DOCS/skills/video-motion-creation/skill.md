# Video & Motion Creation Skill

## Mission

Create original videos from a blank brief to a finished master. The result should feel directed and designed—not like an AI slideshow, generic finance-news package, stock-footage compilation, template montage or auto-captioned clip.

Use this skill for YouTube episodes, vertical Shorts and Reels, explainers, brand films, trailers and motion-led educational campaigns. It extends the AMORDER workflow; it does not replace the existing renderers, publishing connection or repository security rules.

## Default constraints

- English language and broad global audience.
- Natural, measured, confident and professional narration; no robotic cadence, exaggerated hype or clickbait.
- Minimal, modern, realistic and cinematic visual direction with genuine motion.
- Free/open-source-first and local processing where practical.
- No paid subscription, stock asset, hosted AI endpoint or generation credit unless the owner explicitly approves that cost.
- Preserve editable source scenes, scripts, manifests, asset provenance, captions, audio stems and reproducible settings where practical.
- Do not claim a review, license, upload, publication or approval exists unless verified.
- Do not imitate a living creator's exact signature style or reproduce a recognisable copyrighted sequence.

## Required brief

Record the channel/brand, audience, objective, platform, format, subject, central question, one-sentence viewer promise, takeaway, language, narration character, duration, aspect ratio, CTA, source requirements, disclosure, visual direction, rights constraints, available tools/assets and approval status.

When details are missing, use conservative assumptions and record them. Never invent statistics, brand claims, trades, returns, endorsements, people, interviews or results.

## Story and script

Write the idea before generating pictures. Define what the viewer should wonder at the beginning and understand by the end.

A short-form sequence should usually progress through:
1. Hook: immediate, honest visual or question. Avoid a long logo animation.
2. Context: explain why the subject matters.
3. Development: reveal one useful idea at a time.
4. Payoff: answer the question or deliver the promised insight.
5. Close: one relevant CTA, only when appropriate.

Narration must be written for speech. Use natural international English, concise sentences, varied rhythm and purposeful pauses. Check names, figures and pronunciation manually. The voice should sound human and composed, not like an advertisement. Listen to the complete narration, not only a sample.

## Research integrity

Use primary sources first: official data, original research, regulator or government material, standards bodies, company filings and direct statements. Corroborate important or disputed claims. Record title, publisher, URL, publication/access date, the exact claim supported, units, geography, timeframe and caveats.

Separate fact, estimate, forecast, opinion and interpretation. Do not use an LLM answer as sole evidence. Do not fabricate sources, quotations, dashboards, maps, scientific visualisations or events. Footage must not imply a place, time or event that it does not actually depict.

## Visual bible

Create a concise visual bible before building the shot list:

- Palette: use the established AMORDER identity; neutral tones and a restrained accent. Avoid automatic neon, gold, red/green trading clichés or unnecessary glow.
- Lighting: motivated key/fill/rim or practical light, plausible falloff and believable shadows.
- Materials: accurate scale, roughness, reflections and fine detail. Avoid waxy, plastic or over-sharpened results.
- Composition: clear subject hierarchy, deliberate negative space, precise alignment and uncluttered frames.
- Lens language: consistent focal perspective. Wide shots establish space; macro/detail shots reveal something specific.
- Camera: each move must reveal, follow, orient or transition. Use smooth easing and believable inertia; avoid arbitrary zooms and constant drift.
- Depth: meaningful foreground/midground/background, not fake blur applied everywhere.
- Typography: one display family plus one supporting family at most; accurate English glyphs, safe margins and phone-readable sizes.
- Transitions: motivated cuts, match cuts, natural occlusion and restrained dissolves. Avoid template swooshes and gratuitous glitches.

## Shot list and motion

Before rendering, write a timecoded shot list. Each shot must record its purpose, duration, narration beat, framing, camera/object movement, lighting, asset/source ID, rights status, transition and overlay.

Motion should clarify or deepen the idea. Use object animation, tracked camera paths, parallax, diagrams, typography or compositing only when they add meaning. A static still with a slow digital zoom is not a moving-footage substitute. A 3D shot should show actual camera or subject motion.

## Visual coverage standard

For a normal promotional Reel, target at least five distinct moving-footage or original animated setups and eight distinct visual assets, with at least 60% of picture time consisting of rights-cleared moving footage or genuinely animated 2D/3D sequences. A documented editorial exception may be used when the story genuinely calls for a typography-led/data-led film, but stills and static cards must never be falsely counted as motion coverage.

The first 1.5 seconds must contain a meaningful image/action, not a title slate. Do not reuse clips merely to extend runtime. Use a contact sheet and shot manifest to detect repetition.

## Blender and open-source production

Use Blender for procedural scenes, camera rigs, physically based materials, lighting, object motion, motion graphics and compositing. Use FFmpeg/ffprobe for edit finishing, motion-compensated frame interpolation, encoding and full-file decode checks. Use Python/Pillow/SVG for precise graphics. Optional local image/video/speech models require model and output licensing review. Preserve source scripts, model/checkpoint revisions, workflow settings and generated-asset provenance.

Use Blender for high-quality PBR hero scenes, procedural environments, physically grounded geometry, controlled reflections, micro-surface detail, motivated key/fill/rim lighting and restrained depth of field. The production renderer exports native-resolution Blender Eevee scene plates, then composes scene-specific animated 2D motion graphics in FFmpeg at the 30 fps delivery rate: tracking sweeps, orbit markers, moving signal nodes, pulsing bars and pause/bracket motifs. Motion graphics must be meaningful, restrained, safe-area-aware and active throughout the narration; they must not imply measured market data unless sourced. Avoid a still plate with only a slow digital zoom. Use deliberate composition differences between scenes and do not reuse one motion loop as a substitute for a sequence. Pin and smoke-test the installed Blender version.

## Audio

Human-recorded narration is preferred. Local TTS is acceptable only after a full-script audition for naturalness, pronunciation and timing. Do not use a robotic fallback voice simply to finish a job.

Keep dialogue, music, ambience and effects separate where possible. Music and sound must have documented rights for the intended platforms and monetisation. Check clipping, sync, intelligibility and loudness against the actual destination requirements. Listen on headphones and ordinary speakers.

## Asset provenance and AI disclosure

For every external asset, record creator, source URL, license/version, attribution, acquisition date, permitted uses and restrictions. Review fonts, music, voice, footage, model weights and generated content independently.

For AI-generated content, record the model/checkpoint, revision, license, prompt/workflow, seed where available, generation date and review status. Label reconstructions, simulations and illustrative material where viewers could mistake them for evidence. Never fabricate interviews, lip-sync, broker screens, trade execution or market evidence.

## AMORDER editorial safety

A no-trade day is not a failed story. It can become a process-led video about waiting, risk discipline, market uncertainty or why criteria did not align, using only approved public-safe information.

When describing a simulated trade, identify it as paper trading, not live-money performance and not investment advice. Do not disclose private symbol-level research, broker details, fills, positions or performance figures. Never provide buy/sell calls or price targets.

## Automated release and owner authorization

The owner has enabled standing automatic publishing for public-safe AMORDER educational content on the already-connected Buffer destinations. Per-asset human signoff is not required while that authorization remains active. Do not claim that a human watched or approved a video when only automated checks were performed.

Before queueing, the existing AMORDER workflow must pass automated story/factual checks, public-source and rights checks, disclosure and privacy scans, full-file decode, video/audio stream and loudness checks, frame/black/freeze checks, motion-coverage checks, subtitle timing/readability/safe-zone checks, aspect-ratio/duration validation, public HTTPS download verification and duplicate/status checks. Any critical failure, missing evidence or ambiguous result means HOLD/draft. Only the existing AMORDER render/publish path may publish; never activate a competing publisher.
